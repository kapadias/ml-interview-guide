SUMMARY: The ideal artifact is not a reference book but a four-layer performance system: a generative kernel (L0) of ~12 one-pagers that most answers derive from, a bank of ~100 speakable 2-8 minute answer arcs (L1), on-demand deep dives (L2), and a rehearsal/retention layer of drills, mocks, numbers sheets, and war stories (L3) — entered through a 90-minute triage diagnostic. Its knowledge spine is organized as ONE workflow narrative (model → pretrain → post-train → serve → evaluate → apply), cross-indexed by interview round for rehearsal; roles (research/applied/infra) are served by lenses and reweighted tracks over the same spine, not separate material. The minimum effective dose is ~22 hours. The existing book (24 topic chapters, 248 Q&A, ~25k lines of LaTeX at /home/user/deep-learning-essentials) is a strong L2 corpus with unusually good per-question scaffolding (red flags, follow-ups, frequency/difficulty tags) and a proto-triage appendix, but it is organized by the rejected dimension (topic chapters), contains ~20% off-target material for frontier labs (RecSys, GNNs, classic vision), and is missing almost the entire performance layer: L0 kernel pages, the consolidated numbers sheet, war-story templates, mock scripts, self-test protocol, and role tagging. The right move is refactor-and-extend, not rewrite: mine the 248 questions into L1 arcs along a new workflow spine, distill TL;DR/keyinsight boxes into L0, and build L3 new.

# What preparation is FOR (the derivation that fixes everything else)
Interview prep for a Staff engineer is not learning — it is **conversion**: turning latent, unevenly-indexed expertise into fluent spoken performance under follow-up pressure, in 2-6 weeks of fragmented time. Three consequences drive the whole design:

1. **The bottleneck is retrieval-under-pressure, not coverage.** A Staff candidate already knows 70-80% of the content; what fails in the room is fluent access, ordering, and timing. Therefore the artifact must optimize *recall paths* (arcs, chains, drills), not *storage* (encyclopedic chapters). Every page must answer: 'what will come out of my mouth, in what order, in how many minutes?'
2. **Time is the scarcest resource, and its allocation must be diagnostic-driven.** With 25-70 total prep hours, spending them uniformly across 24 topics is malpractice. The artifact must begin with a triage instrument that maps the candidate's *personal* gap profile onto the material, so 80% of hours go to the 20% of material that is weak AND high-frequency.
3. **Staff signal is four distinct competencies needing four distinct formats:** judgment (trade-offs under constraints → decision trees + 'it depends' arc templates), scale intuition (→ memorized numbers tables + estimation drills), experience (→ war-story bank in STAR-for-ML form), and first-principles recovery (→ derivation chains practiced from a blank page). A Q&A box exercises none of these directly; it only exercises recognition.

The design below is derived from these three consequences plus the dependency-structure constraint: a small kernel (~12 ideas: the transformer block, cross-entropy/MLE, backprop as chain-rule bookkeeping, GEMM cost model, memory arithmetic, scaling laws, the bias-variance/regularization cluster, KV-cache algebra, the RL-as-shifted-MLE view of post-training, parallelism taxonomy, quantization number formats, eval-as-measurement-theory) generates first-pass answers to the majority of questions. Kernel first; everything else framed explicitly as an extension of a named kernel idea, so forgotten details are re-derivable in the room.

BOOK COVERAGE: PARTIAL, implicitly. The book's stated philosophy ('interview playbook, not a textbook', per README.md; consistent What-it-is → Key-formula → When-to-use → Pitfalls format; `interviewtest`/`redflags`/`followups` macros in main.tex lines 253-257) shows it already understands interviews as performances — this is its biggest asset. But it operationalizes that understanding only at the single-question grain. There is no triage instrument, no explicit kernel, no notion of recall-path vs. storage, and no treatment of the four Staff competencies as distinct formats: judgment gets one chapter (19, Decision Frameworks), scale numbers are scattered, war stories and derivation-from-blank-page practice are entirely absent.

# Layer architecture: L0 kernel → L1 arcs → L2 deep dives → L3 rehearsal, entered via triage
**Entry gate — T0 Triage Diagnostic (90 min, done once, day 1).** A 60-question rapid self-audit (30 sec/question: 'could I speak 3 minutes on this right now? green/yellow/red') covering all units, plus one 20-minute recorded self-mock. Output: a personal heat map that assigns each L2 unit a priority (skip / skim / deep-dive) and selects which L1 arcs go into the drill rotation. Re-run a 30-min version at the midpoint.

**L0 — Kernel one-pagers (~12 pages, ≤1 page each).** One page per generative idea (list in previous section). Each page: the central equation or picture, the 3-sentence spoken version, the 5 numbers attached to it, and 'what this generates' — pointers to every L1 arc that derives from it. These are the pages re-read every day; they must be printable and fit in ~15 min of daily review. *Build/absorb budget: 4-6 h; maintenance: 15 min/day.*

**L1 — Core-100 answer arcs.** ~100 questions, each formatted as a speakable arc, not prose: **Headline** (1 sentence, the answer) → **Mechanism** (60-90 s, anchored to a kernel page) → **Trade-off/It-depends** (the Staff move) → **A number** (scale anchor) → **Experience hook** (one-line bridge to a war story) → **Follow-up tree** (the 3 most likely follow-ups, one line each). Tagged by round, role, frequency, and target duration (2/5/8 min versions). *Budget: 12-18 h to work through the personal-priority ~60 of them aloud.*

**L2 — Deep-dive chapters (pull, never push).** Full-depth treatments per unit, read ONLY where triage said red/yellow-and-high-frequency. Each L2 section ends by compressing itself back into its L1 arcs ('what you must be able to say from this section'). *Budget: 8-25 h depending on gap profile — this is the layer that flexes with the 2-week vs 6-week runway.*

**L3 — Performance layer (the part almost nobody builds and that matters most at Staff).** (a) Drill decks: numbers flash-drills, 10 derivation chains from blank paper, 8 whiteboard figures drawn from memory, estimation drills ('KV cache for 70B at 128k context, batch 32 — go'). (b) War-story bank: 8-10 stories in STAR-for-ML template, each cross-tagged to the behavioral competencies and to L1 arcs it can garnish. (c) Mock scripts: 6 self-administerable 45-min mocks (one per round type) with interviewer follow-up trees and rubrics. (d) Retention protocol: spaced schedule (D1/D3/D7/D14) applied to L0 pages and weak arcs. *Budget: 10-20 h, front-weighted to the final 10 days.*

**Time budgets by runway** (busy engineer: ~1 h weekdays, ~3 h weekend days):
- **2 weeks (~22-26 h):** T0 1.5 h → L0 5 h → L1 top-40 arcs 8 h → L2 only for red+high-freq 3 h → L3 6 h (numbers, 5 stories, 2 mocks).
- **4 weeks (~45 h):** add: L1 to ~70 arcs, L2 for all yellow units, 4 mocks, full derivation-chain drill set.
- **6 weeks (~65-70 h):** full L1-100, L2 across the spine, 6 mocks including one live with a human, second triage pass.
The ratio to protect: **≥35% of total hours in L3** (speaking, drilling, mocking). Reading past that ratio is procrastination dressed as prep.

BOOK COVERAGE: PARTIAL for L1/L2, ABSENT for T0/L0/L3. L2: well served — 24 chapters, ~25k lines, this is the book's substance. L1: half-served — the 248 `interviewq` boxes have model answers, red flags, tested-signal, and follow-ups (macros at main.tex:253-257), which is 60% of the arc template; missing are the headline-first ordering, timed 2/5/8-min versions, the explicit kernel anchor, the number anchor, and the experience hook. The appendix question index (sections/appendix_question_index.tex) with difficulty/frequency/type organization and 'curated study plans' is a proto-triage and proto-drill-selector — refactorable into T0 and the L1 tag system with modest work. L0: absent as an artifact; raw material exists in the scattered `tldr` and `keyinsight` boxes and `mathresult` environments, which can be distilled into kernel pages. L3: absent entirely — no drills, no mock scripts, no war-story bank, no spaced-repetition protocol, no recorded-self-mock instructions. This is the largest build-new item.

# Organizing dimension: a workflow spine cross-indexed by round (and why pure topic chapters are wrong)
**The argument.** Four candidate organizations:
- *Chapters-by-topic* (attention, losses, optimization, ...) matches how textbooks are written and how knowledge is stored, but NOT how it is retrieved in interviews. It hides the dependency structure (attention, KV cache, and flash-attention live three chapters apart), duplicates shared ideas, and gives no answer to 'what do I read tonight before Thursday's system-design round?'
- *Rounds-by-loop* (coding round, design round, research round, behavioral) matches the delivery format exactly, but produces massive content duplication — the transformer block appears in every round — and rounds vary by company, so the structure is unstable.
- *One workflow narrative* (pretrain → post-train → serve → evaluate) matches how frontier labs actually work in 2026, makes every fact causally connected (memorable, re-derivable), and mirrors the story arc interviewers themselves follow in deep-dives. Its weakness: it under-serves foundations (backprop, losses) that precede the lifecycle, and it doesn't tell you what to rehearse for a given round.
- **Verdict: hybrid — workflow spine for the knowledge layers (L0/L1/L2), round index for the rehearsal layer (L3).** Knowledge is stored once, along the lifecycle of one model ('follow one 2026-scale LLM from birth to production'); rounds are views over that spine. This exploits the dependency structure (constraint 4), reads as a narrative (retention), and still answers 'what do I do before Thursday' (the round view).

**The actual top-level structure, unit by unit:**
- **U0 — Kernel** (the 12 L0 one-pagers; doubles as Part 0 of the narrative: 'the physics the whole lifecycle obeys').
- **U1 — The Model (anatomy of a 2026 LM):** the transformer block in full modern dress — RMSNorm/pre-norm, RoPE and long-context variants, SwiGLU, GQA/MLA, MoE routing; tokenization; parameter-count and FLOPs anatomy; what changed since 2020 and *why* (each change as a fix to a named bottleneck). ~15 L1 arcs.
- **U2 — Pretraining:** data pipeline (dedup, filtering, mixtures, curriculum), objective and loss curves, scaling laws and compute-optimal reasoning, optimization at scale (AdamW, LR schedules, instabilities, loss spikes), distributed training (data/tensor/pipeline/expert/sequence parallelism, ZeRO/FSDP, precision: bf16/fp8, checkpointing, failure recovery, MFU accounting). ~25 arcs. This is the deepest unit for research-eng and infra roles.
- **U3 — Post-training:** SFT and data quality; RLHF pipeline; reward models and their pathologies; DPO/IPO/GRPO family and when each; RL for reasoning (verifiable rewards, test-time compute); safety training, refusals, Constitutional-AI-style methods; catastrophic forgetting and capability regressions. ~18 arcs.
- **U4 — Inference & Serving:** KV-cache algebra, prefill/decode asymmetry, continuous batching, paged attention, quantization (formats, methods, quality cost), speculative decoding, distillation, latency/throughput/cost triangle, serving economics ($/1M tokens from first principles). ~18 arcs.
- **U5 — Evaluation & Reliability:** benchmark design and contamination, elo/pairwise evals, LLM-as-judge pitfalls, statistical significance at eval scale, production monitoring, regression gating, incident stories. ~12 arcs.
- **U6 — Application layer:** RAG and long-context (when each), agents and tool use, fine-tune vs prompt vs RAG decision tree, embeddings/retrieval quality, guardrails. ~12 arcs. Deepest unit for applied roles.
- **UB — Foundations backfill (reference, not narrative):** backprop, losses, regularization, classic optimization, probability — kept OUT of the spine, entered only via triage redirects, because a Staff candidate reads these to patch specific holes, not as a course.
- **R-index — the round views (L3):** R1 ML coding (implement attention/sampler/LoRA from scratch, debug a training script), R2 system design (design: pretraining cluster / serving stack / eval pipeline / RAG system — each maps to one U-unit), R3 research depth (paper walk-throughs, 'defend a design choice' drills, pulling from U2/U3), R4 behavioral/Staff signal (war-story bank), R5 domain deep-dive (role-track dependent). Each R-page is 2 sides: what this round tests, which arcs to rehearse, one mock script, one rubric.

BOOK COVERAGE: The book is organized by the REJECTED primary dimension: 24 topic chapters in 6 parts (main.tex lines 344-399), textbook-shaped even though question-centric in content. However, the mapping onto the workflow spine is mostly a refactor, not a rewrite: U1 ← ch. 5 (Attention & Transformers, 17 Q) + parts of 4, 9, 14; U2 ← ch. 15 (Training & Optimization) + ch. 2 + scaling content in 5/14 — though distributed-training-at-scale depth (parallelism, fp8, MFU, failure recovery) looks thin and likely needs new material; U3 ← ch. 20 (RL & RLHF) + ch. 23 (Safety & Alignment) + ch. 16; U4 ← ch. 22 (Inference Optimization, 12 Q) + ch. 14; U5 ← ch. 18 (Evaluation) + ch. 17 (Production); U6 ← ch. 24 (Long Context & RAG) + ch. 21 (Multimodal) + ch. 6/7 (embeddings/similarity); UB ← ch. 1-4. Off-target for OpenAI/Anthropic-tier 2026: ch. 12 (RecSys, 15 Q), ch. 13 (GNNs, 8 Q), most of ch. 10 (vision) — ~31+ questions (~13%) to demote to an optional annex, freeing prep hours. No narrative connective tissue exists between chapters, and there is no rounds index at all (the appendix indexes by difficulty/type/frequency/topic, not by interview round) — both must be built new, but the frequency/difficulty/type badge system (main.tex lines 227-250) means every question is already metadata-rich enough to be re-indexed mechanically.

# Formats beyond Q&A boxes: the eight artifacts each Staff competency actually requires
Q&A boxes train recognition. Staff interviews test production. Eight formats, each mapped to the failure mode it prevents:

1. **Derivation chains (prevents: freezing when a detail is forgotten).** ~12 canonical chains, each a numbered sequence of 5-9 steps from a kernel idea to a result, designed to be reproduced on a whiteboard from step 1: attention from the retrieval-by-softmax view including the √d scaling; backprop through one block; cross-entropy from MLE and why it pairs with softmax; Adam from momentum+RMS; KV-cache memory from first principles; Chinchilla-style compute-optimal reasoning; LoRA from low-rank-update logic; DPO from the RLHF objective; speculative-decoding acceptance math; MoE FLOPs vs params; fp16 vs bf16 failure modes from float layout; variance of eval metrics / sample-size math. Format: each step is one line + the spoken sentence that accompanies it. Practice protocol: blank page, 8 minutes, compare, repeat on the spaced schedule.
2. **Numbers tables — 'the napkin sheet' (prevents: hand-waving where Staff must estimate).** Two printed pages, memorized: hardware (H100/B200-class FLOPs, HBM size/bandwidth, NVLink/IB bandwidths, rough $/GPU-hr), model arithmetic (params↔bytes by precision, FLOPs ≈ 6ND train / 2ND infer, KV bytes/token formula with 2-3 worked examples), training realities (tokens for 7B/70B-class compute-optimal, typical MFU, batch sizes in tokens), serving realities (tok/s/GPU regimes prefill vs decode, latency budgets, rough $/1M tokens), data (Common-Crawl-scale token counts, dedup ratios). Each row: number + one-line derivation-or-source so it can be re-derived if misremembered.
3. **Decision trees (prevents: unstructured 'it depends').** ~8 one-page trees whose BRANCHES ARE THE SPOKEN ANSWER: fine-tune vs prompt vs RAG; choose parallelism strategy from model size + cluster; quantization method by deployment constraint; DPO vs PPO-style vs rejection sampling; encoder vs decoder vs embedding model by task; buy-API vs self-host by volume/latency/privacy; long-context vs retrieval; eval strategy by launch risk. Each leaf carries the one number or constraint that justifies it.
4. **Whiteboard figure set (prevents: fumbling the marker).** 8 figures practiced to 60-second reproduction: transformer block with tensor shapes at every edge; attention matrix with masking; KV-cache growth diagram; 4-way parallelism grid (which axis splits what); RLHF pipeline boxes-and-arrows; serving stack (router→prefill→decode→cache); loss-curve pathology gallery (spike, divergence, overfit, data repeat); RAG pipeline with failure points annotated. Format: final figure + stroke order + the narration that accompanies each stroke.
5. **Mock-interview scripts (prevents: first-time-live syndrome).** 6 scripts, one per round type, each: opening prompt, a follow-up TREE (if candidate says X, push Y) so they work self-administered, a Staff-calibrated rubric, and a 10-min debrief checklist. Two are designed to be handed to a friend/peer verbatim.
6. **War-story templates — STAR-for-ML (prevents: rambling experience answers).** Template per story: Situation (1 sentence + one scale number), Task (the constraint that made it hard), Action (2-3 decisions, each with the alternative you rejected and why), Result (metric delta + cost/latency/time), Reflection (what you'd do differently — the Staff differentiator). Bank of 8-10 covering the canonical axes: ambiguity, cross-team conflict, technical bet that failed, mentorship/leverage, production incident, influencing without authority, scope-cutting under deadline. Each tagged to the L1 arcs it can be attached to ('when asked about eval, pivot to story #4').
7. **Self-test protocol (prevents: illusion of competence from re-reading).** A one-page standing procedure: daily 15-min blurting session (pick 3 arcs, speak aloud, record, score against headline/mechanism/trade-off/number checklist); spaced schedule D1/D3/D7/D14 for red items; weekly 45-min mock; the rule that no L2 section counts as 'done' until its arcs pass a spoken test.
8. **Answer-arc template + recovery-moves card (prevents: bad arc shape under pressure).** The Headline→Mechanism→Trade-off→Number→Hook skeleton as an explicit card, plus 5 rehearsed recovery moves for the forgotten-fact moment ('let me derive that from the memory budget...', 'I don't recall the constant, but the scaling is...') — first-principles recovery as a practiced behavior, not a hoped-for talent.

BOOK COVERAGE: MIXED — strongest on format 0 (the Q&A box itself: the `interviewq` environment with `stronganswer`/`redflags`/`interviewtest`/`goodgreat`/`followups` structure is genuinely excellent raw material, better than most published prep books). Format 1 (derivation chains): partial — `mathresult` boxes hold whiteboard-worthy results and the README promises formulas 'only if you'd whiteboard it', but they are stated results, not numbered reproducible chains with narration and a blank-page protocol. Format 2 (numbers tables): absent as a consolidated artifact; scale numbers are presumably scattered through chapters 5/14/22 — the napkin sheet must be built new (mostly extraction work). Format 3 (decision trees): partially served by ch. 19 'Decision Frameworks' (8 Q) — the right instinct, likely needs conversion from prose frameworks to one-page trees and expansion to the 2026 LLM decisions. Format 4 (whiteboard figures): partial — TikZ/pgfplots are loaded (main.tex:24-27) so figures exist, but they are drawn for reading; stroke-order + narration + reproduce-from-memory framing must be added. Formats 5 (mock scripts), 6 (war-story templates), 7 (self-test protocol), 8 (arc template/recovery card): entirely absent — the whole behavioral/rehearsal dimension is the book's blind spot; there is no behavioral chapter anywhere in the 24. These four are pure new builds, though format 5 can seed its follow-up trees from the existing `followups` lines.

# Minimum effective dose: the non-skippable ~22 hours
Definition: the smallest set of activities such that skipping any one measurably lowers expected performance across ALL loops at OpenAI/Anthropic-tier, for a candidate who already has Staff-level experience. Sized at **~22 hours ≈ 2 focused weeks for a working engineer.** Anything beyond this is allocated by triage; nothing in this list is allocated by triage — it is unconditional.

1. **T0 triage, full version — 1.5 h.** Skipping it doesn't lower room performance directly, but it corrupts the allocation of every following hour; unconditionally first.
2. **L0 kernel to spoken fluency — 5 h.** All 12 one-pagers, each passing a 3-minute spoken blurt test. This is the load-bearing floor: it is what first-principles recovery draws on when anything else fails.
3. **The Anchor-40 arcs, ALOUD — 8 h.** The 40 highest-frequency arcs (roughly: 8 from U1, 8 from U2, 7 from U3, 7 from U4, 4 from U5, 6 from U6), each spoken at least twice, once recorded. Selection is fixed, not per-candidate — these are the questions whose base rate is high enough that everyone faces most of them. 12 min/arc including one replay.
4. **Napkin sheet memorized + 10 estimation reps — 2 h.** The two-page numbers table to instant recall, then ten 3-minute estimation drills (KV cache sizes, training FLOPs/cost, serving throughput). Scale intuition is the cheapest-per-hour Staff signal in the entire plan.
5. **Six war stories written and spoken — 3.5 h.** Minimum viable behavioral bank: one each for ambiguity, conflict, failure, leverage/mentorship, incident, technical bet. 20 min to write in template + 15 min to speak each. At Staff, the behavioral loop is a veto gate; six stories cover >90% of prompt space via re-angling.
6. **Two full mocks with debrief — 2 h.** One system-design (from the R2 scripts), one behavioral+deep-dive hybrid, both timed, at least one recorded or with a human. The first mock is always the worst performance of the cycle — it must not happen in a real interview.

Explicitly SKIPPABLE at the 2-week horizon (to make the dose credible): all of UB foundations backfill (kernel covers the interview-visible surface), all L2 reading for green-rated units, arcs 41-100, multimodal/vision/RecSys/GNN annex material, derivation chains beyond the 5 embedded in the kernel pages, and mocks 3-6. The MED is deliberately ~65% performance-layer (items 3-6): under maximum time pressure, rehearsal beats coverage, because the marginal interview point comes from delivering known material better, not from knowing more.

BOOK COVERAGE: PARTIAL scaffolding, no sized dose. The appendix question index advertises 'curated study plans' and organizes all 248 questions by difficulty, type, frequency, and topic — the frequency tags (`\freqhigh` etc., main.tex:227-229) mean the Anchor-40 can be extracted mechanically (filter freq-high, dedup by kernel idea, rebalance to the U-unit quotas above, demote RecSys/GNN/vision hits). But the book nowhere defines an hours-denominated floor, contains none of items 2 (kernel), 4 (napkin sheet), 5 (war stories), or 6 (mock scripts), and its sheer mass (~25k lines) actively works against a busy candidate — with no MED fence, the default failure mode is reading Part I linearly and running out of runway around chapter 9. The single highest-leverage refactor of the existing book is a 3-page front-matter insert: the triage instrument, the MED contract, and the Anchor-40 list with page pointers.

# Serving research-eng vs applied vs infra without tripling the material
**Principle: one spine, three lenses, three tracks — never three books.** Empirically ~75% of the material is role-invariant at frontier labs (everyone gets the transformer anatomy, the KV-cache math, the behavioral loop, the eval questions). The remaining 25% differs in DEPTH and ANGLE, not in topic — infra and research candidates both face 'tell me about RLHF', but the follow-ups diverge (reward-model pathologies vs. rollout-generation throughput). Three mechanisms:

1. **Lens boxes (in L1/L2, the main mechanism).** Each major arc and each L2 section carries an optional 3-line box per role: *RE lens* — the follow-ups go toward why-it-works, ablations, what-would-you-try-next; *Applied lens* — toward when-would-you-NOT-use-this, cost, product constraint, eval in the loop; *Infra lens* — toward throughput, memory, failure modes, what-breaks-at-10x. Same mechanism content, three trained pivots. Cost: ~10% extra text, replaces 200% duplication.
2. **Role tracks (a 2-page router each, in front matter).** Each track re-weights the SAME units: **RE track** — U2/U3 to full depth incl. all derivation chains, U4 to arc level, R3 (research depth: paper defense, experiment-design drills) is the heavy round; adds the only substantial role-exclusive module: 'reading and defending experiments' (~6 pages). **Applied track** — U6 to full depth, U3 at DPO/SFT-practitioner level, U2 at arc level only, R2-applied (design a RAG/agent/eval system) is the heavy round; role-exclusive module: product-metric ↔ model-metric translation (~4 pages). **Infra track** — U2-distributed and U4 to full depth incl. all systems figures, U3 at pipeline-shape level, R2-infra (design the training cluster / serving stack) heavy; role-exclusive module: GPU/network/storage failure taxonomy + MFU debugging (~6 pages). Total role-exclusive material: ~16 pages across all three, versus a shared spine of ~150 — a 10% overhead, not 200%.
3. **Tag-driven views everywhere else.** Every L1 arc, drill, mock script, and war-story slot carries role tags [RE|AP|INF|ALL]; the Anchor-40 has three overlay lists that swap ~10 of the 40; the mock-script set is 4 shared + 2 per-role variants of the design round. The napkin sheet is fully shared (infra candidates must know it cold; research and applied candidates win outsized credit for knowing it at all — same page, different bar, which the self-test protocol encodes as different pass thresholds rather than different content).

The anti-pattern to refuse: separate role editions. They triple maintenance, hide the shared kernel, and mis-serve real candidates, who at Staff level are usually interviewing across 2 of the 3 role archetypes simultaneously anyway.

BOOK COVERAGE: ABSENT as a mechanism, but the substrate is half-ready. The book has exactly one audience and one implicit role (generalist MLE, with a noticeable pre-LLM-era applied tilt: RecSys 15 Q, GNN 8 Q, metric learning 12 Q). It has NO role tags — but it already runs a mature badge/tag system (difficulty L5/L6/L7, type, frequency; main.tex:232-250), so adding a fourth badge dimension [RE|AP|INF|ALL] is mechanically cheap, and the appendix index generator can then emit per-role views. Nothing resembling lens boxes exists, though the existing `followups` and `goodgreat` fields are the natural place to graft role-forked follow-ups without new environments. Role tracks/routers must be built new (2 pages each). Depth asymmetries to fix while tagging: the infra track needs new distributed-training and serving-systems material beyond what ch. 15/22 likely hold (parallelism at 2026 scale, fp8, MFU debugging, cluster design); the applied track needs a product-metric translation module (ch. 17/18/19 are adjacent but system-generic); the RE track needs the experiment-defense module, which no chapter approaches. Meanwhile the existing RecSys/GNN/vision depth becomes the annex serving candidates ALSO interviewing outside frontier labs — worth keeping, but explicitly fenced out of all three frontier-lab tracks.
