# 1. Topic map of the monograph (Bruch, "Foundations of Vector Retrieval", arXiv 2401.09350)
**Part I — Introduction (Ch. 1–3)** — *Interview relevance: HIGH.* The problem formalizations, MIPS-vs-NN distinction, curse-of-dimensionality results, and intrinsic dimensionality are the conceptual backbone every staff-level answer should rest on.

- **Ch. 1 Vector Retrieval** (pp. 3–16): sparse vs dense vs hybrid representations; Definition 1.1 (top-k retrieval: poly preprocessing, poly-size index, o(|X|d) query); the three flavors k-NN / k-MCS / k-MIPS and the reductions between them; why inner product is not a metric (no triangle inequality, no "coincidence": u ≠ argmax_v⟨u,v⟩ in general — Theorem 1.1 restores it in the iid zero-mean, d→∞ limit); ε-approximate retrieval (Def 1.2) and recall = |S∩S̃|/k. *HIGH.*
- **Ch. 2 Retrieval Stability in High Dimensions**: Beyer et al.'s instability theorem (Thm 2.1) — if Var[δ(q,X)]/E[δ(q,X)]² → 0 then every point becomes an ε-approximate NN; holds for iid data under any Lp and under inner product; Thm 2.2 — inner products of iid spherically-symmetric points concentrate at 0 with variance ~ ‖X‖²/d. Practical punchline: NN is meaningless on iid synthetic data, but "nearest cluster" remains meaningful on clustered real data. *HIGH.*
- **Ch. 3 Intrinsic Dimensionality**: doubling measure / expansion rate (Karger–Ruhl) and doubling dimension d∘ (a ball of radius 2r covered by 2^{d∘} balls of radius r); expansion rate is fragile (one added point → log m), doubling dimension is robust (subsets inherit it; union of n sets adds log n); sparse vectors with n nonzeros have d∘ ≤ Cn + n log d. *MEDIUM-HIGH* (the vocabulary that explains why ANN works on 768-d embeddings).

**Part II — Retrieval Algorithms (Ch. 4–8)** — *Interview relevance: HIGH overall.*

- **Ch. 4 Branch-and-Bound / Trees** (k-d trees, randomized partition trees, spill trees, cover trees): exact quantitative story of why trees die in high d (certification visits 2^{Ω(d)} points), defeatist search + randomized forests, potential function Φ governing failure probability, spill-tree space blow-up, cover-tree invariants. *MEDIUM* — interviews rarely go deep on trees, but "why not a k-d tree?" is a classic probe and the defeatist/forest idea recurs in Annoy.
- **Ch. 5 Locality Sensitive Hashing**: (r,(1+ε)r,p₁,p₂)-sensitive families; the PLEB reduction; ρ = ln p₁ / ln p₂ with query time O(d·m^ρ) and space O(m^{1+ρ}); concrete families (bit sampling for Hamming, hyperplane/SimHash with collision prob 1−θ/π, cross-polytope, E2LSH via p-stable distributions); asymmetric transforms for MIPS; multi-probe LSH. *HIGH* — LSH is the canonical "do you know the theory" interview topic.
- **Ch. 6 Graph Algorithms**: Delaunay/Voronoi duality and exact-search guarantee for greedy traversal; k-NN graph as its approximation; IP-Delaunay for MIPS (empty Voronoi cones; top-1 only); Kleinberg's small-world result (long-range edges with exponent = lattice dimension → O(log² m) greedy hops); NSW/HNSW as heuristic approximations; RNG/SNG pruning; α-SNG (Vamana/DiskANN) with the only worst-case guarantees among practical graphs (Indyk–Xu: degree O((4α)^{d∘} log Δ), hops O(log_α(Δ/((α−1)ε))), approximation (α+1)/(α−1)+ε). *HIGH* — this is the theory behind HNSW/DiskANN, the default production index.
- **Ch. 7 Clustering (= IVF)**: the two-stage cluster-then-search framework, C = O(√m) rule, routing function as a ranking problem, why it lacks theory yet works (multi-modal real data), MIPS-specific failure modes (norm-imbalanced clusters, spherical KMeans). *HIGH* — IVF is the other production workhorse, and the open problems here are great senior-interview discussion fodder.
- **Ch. 8 Sampling Algorithms for MIPS**: exploiting linearity of inner product; rank-approximation by sampling coordinates ∝ q_t·Σu_t with alias tables (O(d+S) query, S = O((d/Δ)log(m/δ)) samples); BoundedME bandit-style score elimination with O((m√d/ε)·√log(1/δ)) — linear in m, **sublinear in d**, anytime/budgeted. *LOW-MEDIUM* — niche, but the "anytime knob" and sublinear-in-d framing make distinctive L7 talking points.

**Part III — Compression (Ch. 9–10)** — *Interview relevance: HIGH.*

- **Ch. 9 Quantization**: vector quantization as clustering; PQ (subspace decomposition, L·log₂C-bit codes, asymmetric distance computation via lookup tables); OPQ (learned rotation, alternating minimization); Additive Quantization (sum of full-dimensional codewords, beam-search encoding, strictly generalizes PQ); score-aware / anisotropic quantization for MIPS (ScaNN's core result: parallel residual must be penalized more than orthogonal residual). *HIGH* — memory math + ScaNN's loss are prime staff-interview material.
- **Ch. 10 Sketching**: JL lemma and JL transforms (d∘ = Ω(ε⁻² ln(m/δ))); exact bias/variance of inner-product estimates from Rademacher sketches; asymmetric upper/lower-bound sketches for sparse MIPS; threshold sampling (unbiased inner-product sketch with variance ≤ (2/d∘)·max(‖u*‖²‖v‖², ‖u‖²‖v*‖²)). *MEDIUM* — JL numbers are frequently asked; the sparse-sketch material is specialist.

**Part IV — Appendices (A–D)** — *Interview relevance: LOW as content, MEDIUM as toolkit.* Dataset table (GloVe, Deep1B, MS Turing, Sift, Gist, Splade-encoded MS MARCO/BEIR — useful realistic numbers), probability review, concentration inequalities (Markov, Chebyshev, Chernoff, Hoeffding, Bennett — the proof tools if an interviewer pushes into derivations), linear algebra review.

# 2a. Extraction — Problem statements, MIPS reductions, and the curse of dimensionality
### NNS/MIPS formalization and reductions

**2-minute whiteboard.** Top-k retrieval (Def 1.1): given X ⊂ R^d, |X| = m, preprocess in poly(m,d) time into an index of poly(m,d) size so that argmin^(k)_{u∈X} δ(q,u) is answered in **o(md)** — sublinearity is the entire game (exhaustive search is O(md log k)). Three flavors by δ: **k-NN** (L2), **k-MCS** (angular), **k-MIPS** (−⟨q,u⟩). Draw the reduction diagram:
- MCS → MIPS: L2-normalize the corpus; argmax ⟨q,u⟩/‖u‖ = argmax ⟨q, u/‖u‖⟩. (Query norm never matters.)
- NN → MIPS: expand ‖q−u‖² = ‖q‖² − 2⟨q,u⟩ + ‖u‖²; drop ‖q‖²; append one dimension: q′ = [q; −1/2], u′ = [u; ‖u‖²] ∈ R^{d+1}. Then argmin ‖q−u‖ = argmax ⟨q′,u′⟩.
- MIPS → MCS/NN (the direction you need to reuse a cosine/L2 index for dot product!): scale data so max norm ≤ 1, then φ_data(u) = [u; √(1−‖u‖²)] (unit norm), φ_query(q) = [q; 0]. Then ⟨φq(q), φd(u)⟩ = ⟨q,u⟩ and MIPS becomes MCS on the augmented space (Bachrach et al.; Neyshabur–Srebro showed this simple asymmetric transform suffices — no exotic "asymmetric LSH" needed).

Why MIPS is genuinely harder: inner product is not a metric — no non-negativity, no triangle inequality, and **no coincidence**: a point need not maximize inner product with itself (if p = αv, α>1, then ⟨v,p⟩ > ⟨v,v⟩). Some points can *never* be a MIPS answer for any query (empty inner-product Voronoi cone). Theorem 1.1: for iid zero-mean coordinates, coincidence is restored w.h.p. as d→∞ (Chebyshev + union bound; failure prob ≤ m·σ²/(σ²+d²E[Z²]²) → 0).

**Numbers to memorize.** Brute force O(md); reductions cost exactly **+1 dimension** (NN→MIPS) or +1 dimension with norm-rescaling (MIPS→MCS); ε-approximate criterion δ(q,u) ≤ (1+ε)·δ(q,u*); accuracy in practice reported as recall |S∩S̃|/k, not the (1+ε) guarantee.

**How deep interviews go.** L5: state brute-force cost and that ANN trades recall for speed. L6: produce both reduction formulas on the board and explain when normalizing embeddings changes the answer (it changes ranking whenever norms carry signal — recommendation/popularity models). L7: discuss the failure of the naive MIPS→NN transform for *building graphs* (⟨φ(u),φ(v)⟩ between two data points is NOT rank-equivalent to ⟨u,v⟩; Bruch's fix is a per-point sparse augmentation u^(i) ⊕ √(1−‖u^(i)‖²)·e_{d+i} into R^{d+m}, which restores rank-equivalence for all pairs and only raises intrinsic dimension by O(log m)).

### Concentration / curse of dimensionality (Ch. 2)

**2-minute whiteboard.** Theorem 2.1 (Beyer et al. 1999, generalized): if lim_{d→∞} Var[δ(q,X)] / E[δ(q,X)]² = 0, then for every ε>0, P[δ(q,X) ≤ (1+ε)·δ(q,u*)] → 1 — *every* point becomes an acceptable approximate NN, so the NN question itself is unstable (a tiny query perturbation flips the true NN). One-line verification for iid coordinates: for Lp^p distance (or inner product), variance = dσ², squared mean = d²μ², ratio dσ²/(d²μ²) → 0. Proof skeleton: sandwich all distances in [(1−η)E, (1+η)E] with η = ε/(ε+2) via Markov + union bound. Companion result (Thm 2.2): for spherically symmetric iid data and unit query, Var[⟨q,X⟩] = ‖X‖²/d, so inner products collapse to 0.

The two operational consequences: (1) **never benchmark ANN on iid synthetic vectors** — recall numbers there are meaningless; (2) when data is clustered and queries land in clusters, the *cluster-retrieval* problem stays well-posed even when within-cluster NN is unstable — this is the theoretical excuse for IVF (search top-ℓ clusters approximately, scan inside exhaustively).

**Numbers to memorize.** δ_max/δ_min → 1 as d grows for iid data; NN distance among m uniform points in the unit hypercube concentrates at Θ(√d / m^{1/d}) w.p. ≥ 1 − O(2^{−d}) (Lemma 4.1) — note m^{1/d} ≈ 1 for realistic m and d ≥ 100, i.e., your nearest neighbor is nearly as far as a random point.

**How deep interviews go.** L5: "distances concentrate in high dimensions" qualitatively. L6: the variance/mean² criterion and why real embeddings escape it (low intrinsic dimension, cluster structure). L7: connect to evaluation methodology (synthetic vs real collections behave oppositely for the coincidence experiment, Fig 1.3) and to why recall–latency curves are dataset-dependent, not index-intrinsic.

### Intrinsic dimensionality (Ch. 3)

**2-minute whiteboard.** Doubling dimension d∘: every ball of radius 2r, intersected with X, is coverable by 2^{d∘} balls of radius r. Key properties: a k-dimensional flat has d∘ = O(k); subsets inherit d∘; union of n sets has d∘ + log n; **sparse vectors with ≤ n nonzeros in R^d have d∘ ≤ Cn + n·log d** (union of (d choose n) flats). Doubling metrics strictly contain bounded-expansion-rate metrics (d∘ ≤ 4·d_KR); expansion rate is unstable (adding the origin to {r<|u|<2r} blows it to log m). This is the parameter that all the honest guarantees (RP trees, cover trees, DiskANN analysis) are stated in.

**Numbers to memorize.** 2^{d∘} covering constant; sparse bound n·log d; "guarantees scale like (1/m)^{1/d∘} or (4α)^{d∘}, so they are only meaningful when d∘ ≪ d."

**How deep interviews go.** Mostly L7 flavor: use it to explain *why* HNSW gets ~O(log m) behavior on 768-d embeddings that provably can't hold for worst-case 768-d data, and why the same index tuned on one corpus underperforms on another with higher intrinsic dimension.

# 2b. Extraction — LSH and tree methods
### LSH: definition, guarantees, families (Ch. 5)

**2-minute whiteboard.** A family H is (r, (1+ε)r, p₁, p₂)-sensitive if δ(u,v) ≤ r ⟹ P[h(u)=h(v)] ≥ p₁ and δ(u,v) > (1+ε)r ⟹ P[h(u)=h(v)] ≤ p₂ (need p₁ > p₂). Amplify by AND-ing ℓ hashes into one bucket key g = (h₁,…,h_ℓ) (kills false positives) and OR-ing over L independent tables (recovers recall). With ρ = ln p₁/ln p₂, setting **ℓ = log_{1/p₂} m and L = m^ρ** solves the decision problem PLEB ("point location in equal balls") with constant probability (≥ 1 − 1/e for the collision property), scanning at most 4L candidates ⟹ **query O(d·m^ρ), space O(m^{1+ρ})** — sublinear query, superlinear space. Full ε-approximate retrieval = binary search over a geometric grid of radii R = {(1+ε)^i} up to aspect ratio Δ = δ_max/δ_min: ×O(log log_{1+ε} Δ) time, ×O(log_{1+ε} Δ) space.

Families to know cold:
- **Hamming, bit sampling**: h(u) = u_i; p₁ = 1 − r/d, p₂ = 1 − (1+ε)r/d.
- **Angular, hyperplane LSH (SimHash)**: h(u) = sign⟨r,u⟩, r uniform on sphere; collision prob = 1 − θ/π. One line of proof: a random hyperplane separates the two vectors w.p. θ/π.
- **Angular, cross-polytope**: randomly rotate, snap to nearest ±e_i; ρ = (1/(1+ε)²)·(4−(1+ε)²r²)/(4−r²) + o(1) — strictly better than hyperplane; practical via pseudo-rotation HD (Hadamard × random signs), the same trick as FALCONN.
- **Euclidean, E2LSH (Datar et al.)**: h(u) = ⌊(⟨α,u⟩ + β)/r⌋ with α Gaussian (2-stable: ⟨α,u−v⟩ ~ ‖u−v‖₂·Z), β ~ U[0,r]. Adjacent-bucket structure is what makes multi-probe LSH possible (probe neighboring buckets; ~10× fewer tables for recall > 0.9).
- **MIPS**: no symmetric LSH exists in general; apply the asymmetric [u; √(1−‖u‖²)] / [q; 0] transform and reuse any angular family (Neyshabur–Srebro: this suffices; "asymmetric LSH" machinery unnecessary).

**Numbers to memorize.** ρ = ln p₁/ln p₂; L = m^ρ tables; query O(d·m^ρ); space O(m^{1+ρ}); hyperplane collision 1 − θ/π; success constant 1 − 1/e; candidate scan cap 4L; multi-probe ≈ order-of-magnitude table reduction.

**How deep interviews go.** L5: intuition + SimHash. L6: derive ℓ, L from p₁, p₂ and state the space blow-up — the real reason production abandoned vanilla LSH for graphs/IVF. L7: PLEB-to-approximate reduction, ρ as the single quality dial, the MIPS asymmetry argument, and when LSH still wins (streaming/insert-heavy workloads, dedup at extreme scale, distance functions with no good clustering structure, provable guarantees required).

### Trees / branch-and-bound (Ch. 4)

**2-minute whiteboard.** k-d tree: axis-aligned median splits, depth log m, space Θ(m), build Θ(m log m). Search = route to leaf + **backtracking certification** (prune sibling iff ball B(q, best-so-far) doesn't cross the splitting plane). Friedman's O(log m) expected query holds only in fixed low d; in high d the certified search must touch (1 + G(d)^{1/d})^d leaves with G(d) = cube-to-ball volume ratio = 2^d(d/2)!/π^{d/2} ⟹ **2^{Ω(d)} points visited** — for d ≥ 30 you're doing exhaustive search with extra steps (95% of query time is backtracking). Fixes: drop certification (**defeatist search**, wrong near boundaries) + randomize + build a **forest**: RP trees split on a random direction at a random β ∈ [1/4,3/4] fractile; failure prob per tree ≈ Σ_levels Φ·ln(2e/Φ), where Φ(q,X) = (1/m)Σᵢ ‖q−x₍₁₎‖/‖q−x₍ᵢ₎‖ ∈ (0,1] measures query hardness (Φ→1 ⟺ unstable NN); under a doubling measure Φ_s ≲ (2/s·ln(1/δ))^{1/d∘} — failure can be driven arbitrarily low iff intrinsic dimension is low. **Spill trees** duplicate boundary points into both children (overlap fraction α), removing the boundary failure at space cost O(m^{1/(1−log(1+2α))}). **Cover trees**: leveled structure with nesting/covering (2^ℓ balls)/separation invariants; O(m) space; guarantees in doubling metrics; this is spillage/covering vocabulary that recurs everywhere.

**Numbers to memorize.** 2^{Ω(d)} certification cost; "trees fine to d ≈ 20–30"; RP-tree failure ∝ (1/m∘)^{1/d∘}; spill-tree space exponent 1/(1−log(1+2α)); Annoy = RP-forest with defeatist search (practical anchor).

**How deep interviews go.** Usually one probe: "why doesn't a k-d tree work for 768-d embeddings?" A staff answer gives the ball-crosses-every-hyperplane argument (NN radius √d vs cell side, so Ω(d) levels are ambiguous ⟹ 2^d cells) and then pivots: randomization + forests + no certification (Annoy) buys back practicality, with failure probability controlled by intrinsic, not ambient, dimension.

# 2c. Extraction — Graph indexes, clustering/IVF, sampling
### Graph indexes: HNSW/DiskANN and their theory (Ch. 6)

**2-minute whiteboard.** Base algorithm: nodes = vectors; greedy best-first search with a size-k priority queue (beam); stop when the queue stabilizes. The exact-search gold standard is the **Delaunay graph** (dual of the Voronoi diagram): greedy traversal on any supergraph of it returns the exact top-k for proper metrics (Thm 6.1/6.4), and it is the *minimal* such graph (Thm 6.2). Two fatal problems in high d: construction is exp(d), and the graph degenerates toward complete (distance concentration ⟹ almost all Voronoi regions touch). So all practical graphs approximate it from two directions:
1. **Add long-range edges for speed** — Kleinberg: on a lattice, long-range links drawn with P ∝ dist^{−α} give polylog greedy routing **only when α = lattice dimension** (α=2 in 2-D ⟹ O(log² m) hops; any other α is polynomial: m^{(2−α)/3} or m^{(α−2)/(α−1)}). Beaumont extended this to Delaunay + harmonic-radius long links: O(log² Δ) hops. NSW (Malkov 2014) approximates this by inserting points in random order and linking each to its k nearest current nodes — early edges become the long links; **HNSW** replaces that with an explicit layer hierarchy (geometrically decimated levels, greedy from the top). No guarantees survive the heuristics.
2. **Prune edges for degree** — RNG rule: keep (u,v) iff no w is simultaneously closer to both. **Vamana/DiskANN's α-pruning**: while building u's neighbor list in increasing distance order, discard candidate w if δ(u,w) > α·δ(w,v) for an already-kept v (α > 1 keeps *more* edges than RNG). Indyk–Xu proved this is the one practical graph with worst-case guarantees: max degree O((4α)^{d∘}·log Δ), and greedy search reaches an ((α+1)/(α−1) + ε)-approximate top-1 in O(log_α(Δ/((α−1)ε))) hops; other popular SNG heuristics (HNSW, NSG) have contrived instances forcing linear scans. Practical Vamana: start from a random R-regular graph, insert by searching-then-pruning (the exact algorithm is O(m³)).

MIPS on graphs: inner-product Voronoi regions are cones through the origin and can be **empty** (never-answer points, prunable); IP-Delaunay guarantees top-1 MIPS only — or use the rank-preserving per-point augmentation to R^{d+m} and reuse ordinary L2 graphs (intrinsic dimension only +O(log m)).

**Numbers to memorize.** Greedy hop count O(log² m) in the ideal small-world model; DiskANN approx factor (α+1)/(α−1) (α=1.2 ⟹ 11 — a *weak* worst-case bound; practice is far better) and degree O((4α)^{d∘} log Δ); HNSW knobs M ≈ 16–64 (degree), efSearch/efConstruction = beam width, memory = vectors + ≈ M·4–8 bytes of edges per node; graph indexes = best recall–QPS but highest RAM and slowest builds; complexity claims are empirical, not proven.

**How deep interviews go.** L5: describe HNSW layers + greedy descent. L6: the RNG/α-pruning rule, why the beam (ef) is the recall knob, memory accounting, deletes/updates as the weak spot (FreshDiskANN), filtered search breaking traversal (Filtered-DiskANN). L7: Delaunay optimality + minimality, Kleinberg's α = dimension coincidence, Indyk–Xu positive/negative results, and the argument for why HNSW's hierarchy is arguably redundant on high-d data (long-range links matter mostly in low intrinsic dimension).

### Clustering / IVF and routing (Ch. 7)

**2-minute whiteboard.** Index: cluster X into C clusters (KMeans; **C = O(√m)** typical); store an inverted list per centroid. Query: rank centroids by δ(q, μᵢ), scan the top ℓ (= nprobe) lists exhaustively. Cost = C·d (routing) + ℓ·(m/C)·d (scan); with balanced clusters C ≈ √(m·ℓ) minimizes it — hence √m. Interpretation 1: coarse quantization — you approximate δ(q,u) by δ(q,μ_{cluster(u)}), which is exactly vector quantization (bridge to Ch. 9). Interpretation 2: by Ch. 2, on clustered data "nearest cluster" is stable even when NN isn't — the only theoretical justification IVF has; there is **no formal recall analysis** (unique among the four families). Failure modes worth naming: variable norms + KMeans ⟹ giant clusters of small-norm points and singleton large-norm clusters (use spherical KMeans for MIPS/cosine); centroid = mean is a first-order sketch of a cluster — routing is really a ranking problem (learnable; higher-order per-cluster statistics; open research). IVF composes with everything: route with a graph over centroids, compress lists with PQ (IVF-PQ), spill points into multiple clusters.

**Numbers to memorize.** C = √m (e.g., m=1B ⟹ C ≈ 31K–65K, common FAISS practice 2^16–2^18 with a second-level index over centroids); nprobe is the recall knob (recall vs %-of-clusters-probed curves rise steeply then flatten, Fig 7.2); routing overhead C·d per query.

**How deep interviews go.** L5: the three-step recipe + nprobe trade-off. L6: derive √m; imbalance and norm pathologies; IVF-PQ layout with residual encoding (quantize u − μᵢ inside each list — smaller residual energy ⟹ lower PQ error). L7: routing-as-learning-to-rank (train router on query distribution, MRR surrogate losses), spillage, and IVF-as-LSH conjecture (open).

### Sampling algorithms for MIPS (Ch. 8)

**2-minute whiteboard.** Inner product is linear ⟹ you can estimate the *ranking* without computing scores. (1) Rank approximation (wedge sampling): sample dimension t w.p. ∝ q_t·Σ_u u_t (query-time, O(d) to set up), then sample point u w.p. ∝ u_t (precomputed alias tables, O(1)/sample); frequency of being sampled ≈ inner-product rank; handle negatives by adding sign(q_t u_t). S = O((d/Δ)·log(m/δ)) samples identify the top-1, where Δ = normalized gap between best and runner-up. (2) Score approximation (BoundedME, bandit-style): iteratively sample dimensions without replacement, keep partial dot products, halve the candidate set at the ~median each round; total O((m√d/ε)·√log(1/δ)) — **linear in m but sublinear in d**, the opposite regime of every index in Ch. 4–7. Both are **anytime**: the sample count is a live latency↔accuracy knob at query time, and there is essentially no index build cost.

**Numbers to memorize.** O(d + S) query for wedge sampling; O(m√d/ε ·√log 1/δ) for BoundedME; "harder query = smaller score gap Δ = more samples."

**How deep interviews go.** Rare in interviews; deploy it as an L7 differentiator when asked about strict per-query latency budgets, or huge-d/small-m regimes where graph/IVF overheads dominate.

# 2d. Extraction — Quantization (PQ/OPQ/AQ/ScaNN) and sketching
### Quantization (Ch. 9)

**2-minute whiteboard.** Vector quantization: KMeans with C codewords; each vector stored as log₂C bits; total space O(Cd + m·log₂C); distances via a per-query table of C entries (built in O(Cd)) then m lookups. Lloyd-optimality = exactly the KMeans conditions. **Product Quantization**: split d dims into L subspaces of d∘ = d/L dims; run KMeans with C codewords (almost always C = 256 = 1 byte) in each; code = L bytes. Quantization error decomposes as the sum over subspaces ⟹ L independent KMeans. **ADC (asymmetric distance computation)**: per query build L tables of C entries — table i maps j ↦ ‖S_i q − μ_{i,j}‖² (cost O(LCd∘) = O(Cd)) — then each database point costs **L table lookups + adds**: total O(Cd + mL) vs O(md) exact. Keep the query un-quantized (asymmetric) — only the data-side error enters. **OPQ**: pre-rotate by learned orthogonal R to balance energy/decorrelate subspaces; alternate {fix R, learn PQ} / {fix codebook, solve min_R ‖RU − Ũ‖_F s.t. RRᵀ=I} (closed-form Procrustes). **Additive Quantization**: L codebooks of full-dimensional codewords, ũ = Σᵢ μ_{i,cᵢ} — same L·log₂C bits, strictly generalizes PQ (PQ = AQ with subspace-sparse codewords), lower error; but encoding is a combinatorial search (beam search over L² candidate tuples per round), and L2 distances need ‖u‖² stored since cross-terms ⟨μᵢ,μⱼ⟩ appear; inner-product tables still work: ⟨q,ũ⟩ = Σ⟨q, μ_{i,cᵢ}⟩. Residual/coarse+fine: quantize u − μ_coarse inside IVF lists (IVF-PQ / inverted multi-index).

**MIPS-aware (ScaNN) quantization**: the right objective is E_q[⟨q, u−ũ⟩²] = (u−ũ)ᵀ E[qqᵀ] (u−ũ) — reconstruction MSE is only correct for isotropic queries. Guo et al. 2020 add a score weight ω(⟨q,u⟩) = 1_{s≥θ} (only care about points that could win) and prove the loss splits over the residual r = u−ũ decomposed parallel/orthogonal to u: ℓ ∝ h∥·‖r∥‖² + h⊥·‖r⊥‖² with **h∥ ≥ h⊥ always** — i.e., preserving the *norm/parallel* component matters more than the angle, because high-norm points can win MIPS even at large angles. In high d the weight ratio concentrates: minimize Σ_u [(θ/‖u‖)²/(1−(θ/‖u‖)²)]·‖r∥(u,ũ)‖² + ‖r⊥(u,ũ)‖² with a modified Lloyd iteration. This anisotropic loss is the core of ScaNN; extensions: anisotropic AQ (Zhang 2022), query-aware weights (Zhang 2023).

**Numbers to memorize (the memory math).** fp32 768-d = 3,072 B/vector; 1B vectors = ~3 TB raw, 1.5 TB fp16. PQ with L=96, C=256 ⟹ 96 B/vector (32× compression); 1B ⟹ ~96 GB + codebooks (L·C·d∘ floats = 256·d floats ≈ 786K floats ≈ 3 MB — negligible) + IDs. SQ8 = d bytes (4×). ADC cost per point: L lookup-adds (e.g., 96 vs 768 multiply-adds; SIMD 4-bit variants push this much further). Bits per vector = L·log₂C. Standard staff-level recipe for billion-scale in RAM: IVF + OPQ/PQ codes + exact-vector re-rank of the top few hundred (vectors on disk — DiskANN's layout).

**How deep interviews go.** L5: PQ compression arithmetic. L6: ADC mechanics, symmetric-vs-asymmetric error, OPQ motivation, residual encoding in IVF-PQ, and the compensate-with-reranking pattern. L7: AQ vs PQ trade (error vs encoding cost), the anisotropic-loss derivation sketch and *when* it matters (MIPS with spread-out norms; it reduces to MSE when norms are constant), quantifying recall loss as a function of code length.

### Sketching (Ch. 10)

**2-minute whiteboard.** JL lemma: any m points embed into d∘ = O(ε⁻²·ln(m/δ)) dims with all pairwise distances distorted ≤ (1±ε) — **independent of d**. Achieved by linear maps: Gaussian N(0, 1/d∘) entries, or ±1/√d∘ Rademacher; fast versions use Hadamard mixing. For inner products: ⟨Φu,Φv⟩ is an **unbiased** estimator of ⟨u,v⟩ with |⟨Φu,Φv⟩−⟨u,v⟩| ≤ ε‖u‖‖v‖ w.h.p., and exact variance (1/d∘)(‖u‖²‖v‖² + ⟨u,v⟩² − 2Σᵢuᵢ²vᵢ²) → error is relative to the *norm product*, not the inner product — brutal for near-orthogonal (e.g., sparse) pairs. Data-oblivious ⟹ distribution-shift-proof and analyzable, unlike learned codebooks (the fundamental sketching-vs-quantization trade). Two sparse-specific alternatives: asymmetric upper/lower-bound sketches (hash active coordinates into d∘/2 buckets keeping max/min per bucket via h maps; query stays raw; always overestimates — usable as an admissible bound for pruning), and threshold sampling (keep coordinate i w.p. min(1, d∘·uᵢ²/‖u‖²), reweight at scoring: unbiased, Var ≤ (2/d∘)·max(‖u*‖²‖v‖², ‖u‖²‖v*‖²) where * restricts to shared nonzeros — beats JL when overlap is small).

**Numbers to memorize.** d∘ = ε⁻²·ln m rule of thumb (ε=0.1, m=10⁷ ⟹ d∘ ≈ 100·16 ≈ 1,600; ε=0.2 ⟹ ~400): JL is dimension-*independent* but ε⁻² is punishing — this is why 64–128-d PQ-style learned compression beats random projection at equal budget on real data, and why random projection is the fallback when data is adversarial/drifting.

L7: sparse-vector failure of JL, asymmetric bounding sketches for pruning, sampling sketches, and the oblivious-vs-learned compression decision under distribution shift.

# 4. New interview questions supported by this material (books' style: title + answer sketch + level/archetype)
1. **"Estimate the memory footprint of a 1B-vector semantic search index and get it under 128 GB."** — L5, Estimation. Sketch: raw fp32 768-d = 3 TB, fp16 1.5 TB — hopeless in RAM. SQ8 → 768 GB; PQ with 96 subquantizers × 256 codewords → 96 B/vector = 96 GB + negligible codebooks (~3 MB) + IDs/graph. Land on IVF-PQ (add ~C·d centroid storage, C≈√m) or PQ codes in RAM + exact vectors on SSD for reranking (DiskANN layout). Red flag: forgetting HNSW's edge overhead (~M×4–8 B/node) or proposing HNSW-over-raw-vectors at this scale.

2. **"Your ANN benchmark on synthetic Gaussian vectors shows terrible recall at any latency, but the same index on real embeddings is fine. Explain."** — L6, Debugging. Sketch: iid data ⟹ distance concentration (Var/mean² → 0), δ_max/δ_min → 1, every point is a near-tie for NN; the problem, not the index, is ill-posed — and intrinsic dimension equals ambient dimension so no structure to exploit. Real embeddings live near a low-dimensional, clustered manifold. Conclusion: benchmark on real or realistically-structured data; distrust papers that win on uniform vectors.

3. **"A rec-sys team L2-normalizes item embeddings from a dot-product-trained two-tower model to reuse a cosine HNSW index. Ranking quality drops. Diagnose and fix."** — L6, Debugging. Sketch: normalization erases norms, which encode popularity/quality in dot-product-trained models — MIPS ≠ MCS. Fix: the augmentation reduction (scale so max ‖u‖ ≤ 1; index [u; √(1−‖u‖²)], query [q; 0]) makes any cosine/L2 index exact for MIPS; or use an IP-native index. L7 depth: note this transform is query-to-data only; graph *construction* on transformed points distorts data-data ranking, motivating IP-graph work or per-point augmentation.

4. **"Why doesn't a k-d tree work for 768-dimensional embeddings, and what minimal changes rescue tree indexes?"** — L5, Trade-off/Concept. Sketch: exact search needs backtracking certification; NN radius ≈ √d ≈ cell diameter, so the query ball crosses splits in Ω(d) dimensions ⟹ 2^{Ω(d)} leaves visited ≈ exhaustive. Rescue: drop certification (defeatist search), randomize split directions/thresholds, build a forest (RP trees / Annoy); failure probability then scales with *intrinsic* dimension, ≈ (1/leaf-size)^{1/d∘}.

5. **"Given an LSH family with p₁ = 0.8, p₂ = 0.5 for your target radius, size the index for 100M vectors and decide whether to use it."** — L6, Estimation/Trade-off. Sketch: ρ = ln 0.8/ln 0.5 ≈ 0.32; ℓ = log₂(10⁸) ≈ 27 hashes/table; L = m^ρ ≈ 10^{2.6} ≈ 380 tables; query O(d·m^ρ) is fine but space O(m^{1+ρ}) ⟹ ~380 copies of the ID set — likely prohibitive. Mitigations: multi-probe (≈10× fewer tables), better family (cross-polytope lowers ρ). Then the honest call: for static in-RAM corpora, graphs/IVF dominate empirically; LSH wins when you need guarantees, cheap incremental inserts, or hashing as a routing/sharding primitive.

6. **"Design billion-scale ANN when the vectors don't fit in RAM."** — L6/L7, Design. Sketch: DiskANN pattern — compressed (PQ) codes + graph adjacency in RAM for navigation, full-precision vectors on SSD touched only for the beam's candidates; α-pruned graph keeps hops O(log Δ)-ish so SSD reads stay bounded; final exact rerank of top-100. Alternatives: sharded HNSW (RAM cost), IVF-PQ (cheaper build, lower recall ceiling). Discuss update path (FreshDiskANN-style merge) and per-stage recall monitoring.

7. **"Your HNSW recall@10 drops from 0.95 to 0.6 when users apply a metadata filter matching 2% of the corpus. Why, and what are the fixes?"** — L6, Debugging. Sketch: post-filtering top-k leaves <k survivors; pre-filtering breaks the graph — the subgraph induced by 2% of nodes is disconnected/unnavigable, greedy search strands in local optima. Fixes: oversample k (k/selectivity) with post-filter; filter-aware traversal that walks the full graph scoring only matching nodes (Filtered-DiskANN); per-tenant/per-partition indexes for hot filters; brute-force fallback below a selectivity threshold (2% of 10M × d flops may be cheaper than a broken graph walk).

8. **"Walk me through how a PQ index computes distances without decompressing, and where the time goes."** — L5, Concept/Whiteboard. Sketch: per query, build L lookup tables (table i: C entries ‖S_i q − μ_{i,j}‖², cost O(Cd)); each candidate then costs L lookup-adds instead of d multiply-adds (768 → 96); total O(Cd + mL). Asymmetric (query un-quantized) halves the error vs symmetric. Mention SIMD 4-bit fast-scan as the production refinement, and that PQ error is why you rerank the top few hundred with exact vectors.

9. **"When and why does ScaNN's anisotropic quantization beat plain PQ?"** — L7, Trade-off. Sketch: reconstruction MSE is the right objective only for isotropic queries; for MIPS what matters is E_q[⟨q, u−ũ⟩²], and weighting by score (ω = 1_{⟨q,u⟩≥θ}) provably makes the residual component *parallel* to u (norm distortion) cost more than the orthogonal one (h∥ ≥ h⊥). Wins when norms vary and the task is dot-product retrieval (recs, DPR-style QA); collapses to ordinary MSE when norms are constant (cosine on normalized vectors ⟹ don't bother).

10. **"You must cut embedding storage 6× before indexing. Random projection (JL) or learned compression (PQ/OPQ/Matryoshka)? Give the math."** — L6, Trade-off/Estimation. Sketch: JL needs d∘ = O(ε⁻² ln m) — for m=10⁸, ε=0.15 that's ≈ 2,000·0.82 ≈ 800+ dims, i.e., no savings from 768; its error is relative to ‖u‖‖v‖ (bad for near-orthogonal pairs), though it's unbiased, data-oblivious, and immune to distribution shift. Learned compression exploits data structure for far better rate–distortion but drifts stale. Answer: PQ/OPQ (+ rerank) for a stable corpus; JL/sketching when data is adversarial, streaming, or shifting, or as a pre-transform.

11. **"Why does greedy walk on a proximity graph find the nearest neighbor at all? What breaks in high dimensions, and what's the one graph with worst-case guarantees?"** — L7, Concept. Sketch: on any supergraph of the Delaunay graph, every local optimum of greedy search is global (Voronoi duality); Delaunay is also the minimal such graph. In high d it's unbuildable and near-complete, so practice approximates: k-NN edges + long-range links (Kleinberg: link prob exponent must equal the dimension for polylog routing) + RNG-style pruning. Vamana's α-pruning is the exception with proofs: degree O((4α)^{d∘} log Δ), O(log_α Δ) hops to an ((α+1)/(α−1)+ε)-approximate answer; HNSW/NSG have adversarial linear-time instances.

12. **"Tune an IVF index: how many clusters for 100M vectors, and what do you do when p99 latency is fine but recall plateaus as you raise nprobe?"** — L6, Design/Debugging. Sketch: C ≈ √(m·nprobe) balancing routing C·d vs scan nprobe·(m/C)·d ⟹ C ~ 10⁴–10⁵ (then index the centroids themselves with HNSW). A recall plateau in nprobe means the *router* is the bottleneck, not the scan: centroid-mean sketches misroute queries — causes include imbalanced clusters from variable norms (fix: spherical KMeans / normalize), boundary queries (fix: cluster spillage / soft assignment), or train/serve query-distribution mismatch (fix: learned routing trained as ranking, or re-clustering). Also verify recall is measured against exact top-k with the *same* metric.
